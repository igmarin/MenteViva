# Sound & haptics — decision record (#26)

Jev asked 6 questions against the audience/constraints state; raw pair archived
in `jev-json/006-sounds.*`. Model served: jev-1.13.0.

## Decisions (deterministic, adopted)

1. **Sound is a net positive** (Jev 0.83) → ship it, but soft and always
   switchable off.
2. **Soft, low-frequency tones** (0.87), never sharp high beeps — age-related
   hearing loss keeps low frequencies longer, and sharp beeps startle.
3. **Synthesize in code** with `AVAudioEngine` (0.75, custom > stock). This also
   removes the licensing question entirely: no audio files, no royalties, no
   fees, no asset pipeline.
4. **Palette of exactly three** (0.74): success (soft two-note rise),
   completion (three-note), tap (single soft tick). A missed answer gets a short
   low, quiet tone — warm-failure copy extends to audio: no harsh buzz.
5. **On by default** (0.62) with a single "Sound & haptics" toggle in Settings.
   One switch for both: two controls is one too many for this audience.
6. **Redundant cues — we override Jev** (Jev said 0.39, i.e. no). Accessibility
   is a floor, not a vote: nothing conveys information by sound alone. Every
   sound is paired with the existing visual state and a haptic.

Haptics are the same principle: `UIImpactFeedbackGenerator` / `UINotificationFeedbackGenerator`, generated by the OS, no assets, no fees.

## Regulation — answered from research, not Jev

- **There is no dedicated regulation on app sounds.** The FDA/FTC medical-claims
  rules bind *marketing claims* (research §1), not beep frequencies. Sound adds
  zero regulatory exposure beyond the claim ceiling we already hold.
- What actually applies is **accessibility guidance**, not law: don't rely on
  sound alone (WCAG SC 1.1.1/1.3.3), avoid startling audio (sensory sensitivity,
  hyperacusis, tinnitus), respect the device's silent switch and volume
  (`AVAudioSession` `.ambient`), and provide an off switch.

## What else we asked (and answered) to stay deterministic

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Quiet how? | Peak amplitude ≈ 0.5 of a system alert; envelope with soft attack/decay |
| A missed-answer sound? | Yes but kind: low (~180 Hz), short, quiet — not a buzzer |
| Haptics independent of sound? | No — one toggle governs both; simpler |
| Does sound block the UI? | Never — played off the main path, fire-and-forget |
| Silent switch? | Respected — `.ambient` session |

## Warmer tones (#240, 2026-10)

Finding 6 of `docs/review-2026-10-gamefeel.md`: the tones were thin blips and the miss tone sat below
what an iPhone speaker reproduces. The note tables and the render moved into the pure `ToneSynth`
(`Services/ToneSynth.swift`); `SoundPlayer` only plays. Tests render every event and check the peak.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Envelope | 10 ms attack, linear fall to zero at 25% of the note (a 25-45 ms blip) | 10 ms attack, exponential decay over the whole note (e^-5 at the end, no hard zero) |
| Timbre | pure sine | sine + second harmonic at 20% |
| Miss tone | 180 Hz, 0.16 s, 0.28 | 280 Hz, 0.20 s, 0.28 (still the lowest and softest) |
| Tap / success / complete | unchanged pitches and levels | same pitches and lengths |
| Peak | at most 0.5 | at most 0.6 (0.5 x 1.2), tested to stay under 0.8 |

Not decided by a test: how it sounds. The owner listens on a device before merging.
